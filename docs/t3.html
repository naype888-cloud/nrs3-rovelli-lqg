NRS³·T³ · Rovelli — area quanta on the three-torus

Same ladder of areas A(j) = 8 π γ lp² √(j (j + 1)), j ∈ ½ ℕ, now on T³: all three axes are closed rings of d ≥ 3 positions. The angle of a ring, θ°(d) = arccos(1/C°(d)), comes from the maximal-current state of the pair (T°, P). It is already 26.57° at d = 3 (light 4/5, dark 1/5) jumps to 49.50° at d = 4, peaks near 46.8° around d = 9 and comes down to 45° in the Szegő limit of the ring (light = dark = ½). Unlike the open axis, the closed one has no ceiling below its limit: the approach is not monotone. The open cube had 0 at d = 3 and the ceiling 28.30°; this page is the closed one. The open page stays at index.html.

The cut of the torus is 2·dy·dz punctures

Cut the ring of the x axis at one place: each of the dy·dz lines crosses the cut twice, because a closed axis has two seams. The 2·dy·dz boundary steps are punctures of spin ½ (two states each), and Bekenstein–Hawking fixes γ = log 2 / (π√3) as on the cube. The horizon of the de Sitter solution of the cut has the area a²·2·dy·dz, with Λ = 3π / (2 ℓ² dy dz log 2).

Scope

Proved here: the LQG area spectrum and puncture counting as stated, and the NRS³ open-axis ceiling restated from the base repository. The NRS³ core is the base repository and does not depend on this page. The readings below — granularity as dimensional uncertainty, the ring limit — are proposals, not results of NRS³: developing them is work for quantum information and quantum gravity.

In NRS³·T³ (proposal)

1. Granularity = dimensional uncertainty of a ring: θ°(d) is positive from d = 3 (26.57°), above 45° from d = 4, and tends to 45°; in the open cube the first two sizes were flat at 0.
2. Area quanta: the seam punctures carry the lowest quantum A(1/2) each; the cut has 2·dy·dz of them, so entropy and area are proportional with the one Immirzi value.
3. The limit: the 45° of the ring limit is half light, half dark; no finite ring attains it, and rings of 4 to 60 positions sit above it (46–49.5°), so there the angle is not a bound.

Source text: Carlo Rovelli, La realidad no es lo que parece (2017), Ch. 5 (quantum space-time), Ch. 6 (area and volume quanta, spin networks) and Ch. 10 (black-hole entropy by counting punctures).
